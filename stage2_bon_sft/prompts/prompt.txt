## Output Requirements

1. Output ONLY a reasoning monologue as an analyst. Keep concise but detailed. Vary sentence structures to avoid repetition.
2. Analyze general user preferences (genres, themes, attributes, motivations)
   and engagement patterns based on history.
3. Express potential interests or tendencies rather than deterministic
   conclusions or single outcomes.
4. Adapt depth to history length: brief key observations for short histories;
   step-by-step tracing of interest shifts for longer ones. Base predictions on
   observed patterns.
5. CRITICAL: Always use ONLY the SID format when referring to items. Never use
   titles, names, or `Item SID:` prefixes. The item descriptions above are
   provided so that you understand what each Semantic ID means -- absorb that
   meaning into how you talk about the Semantic ID, but never write the title
   itself.
6. Never mention 'reference item' or imply knowledge of the target. Reason as
   if predicting blindly.
7. Start directly with reasoning. Do NOT predict a specific next item. End with
   a non-deterministic summary of likely interests (e.g., `may enjoy`, `tends
   to prefer`).


## Auxiliary Labels

Alongside the reasoning, assign the transition_type is bookkeeping only and must not change how the reasoning is
written.

### transition_type

- repeat:
  The next item is essentially the same choice as a specific historical item,
  such as the same item, a close variant, or a replenishment.

- exploit:
  The next item is a distinct standalone choice that continues an established
  interest, preference, category, activity, or goal.

- complement:
  The next item is selected mainly because it supports, enhances, or is used
  together with a specific historical item.

- explore:
  The next interaction moves toward a meaningfully new interest direction that
  is not sufficiently explained by established historical preferences. 

## Reasoning Requirements

Write 4-8 sentences, roughly 120-180 words, in this progression:

1. Evidence: describe what the user's most predictive interactions reveal
   about them. Refer to those items by Semantic ID only, characterized through
   the concrete attributes their descriptions reveal. Read the attributes out
   of the descriptions, but never write the title itself -- attach the meaning
   to the Semantic ID. Make "the user" (or "they"/"their") the grammatical
   subject of most sentences; do not open sentences with report-style framing
   such as "The strongest signal is ...", "The clearest signals come from ...",
   or a bare Semantic ID.
2. Interest: the concrete active interest or need those items support.
3. Mechanism: why that interest plausibly transitions toward a new intent now.
4. Prediction: the attribute profile the user may gravitate toward next, stated
   as a tendency rather than a specific item.
